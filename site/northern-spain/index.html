4 – 17 September 2027 · Madrid · Asturias · Cantabria · Basque Country

Green Spain, Slowly

A Madrid weekend, a fast train under the mountains (with a Roman aqueduct on the way, if we like), four nights in the cider capital, 40,000-year-old hands on a cave wall, a whole turbot over coals, and the Basque coast to finish — urban nights, village days, and the best unpretentious eating in Europe the entire way.

Mum & Pa fly in from Singapore via Frankfurt · X & E from Newark · Sat 4 to Fri 17 September 2027

How we'll travel: urban nights, village days

Every base is a compact, walkable city; the fishing towns, mountain lakes, and stone villages are collected on day trips and transfer days. No car at all until Oviedo — Madrid is done on foot, and a fast train slips under the mountains in about three hours. Then one car, three gentle day trips from one hotel, and two driving days that are really sightseeing days with luggage in the boot. Nobody backtracks, and the group splits exactly once, at a twenty-minute airport run.

Saturday 4 September — fly into Madrid

Taxi to the hotel · everyone together by early evening
Details

X & E land from Newark around lunchtime; Mum & Pa come down from Frankfurt the same afternoon. A flat-fare taxi takes everyone straight to the old town. Nobody drives, nobody navigates. An easy first evening of tapas.

Madrid — 2 nights

A deliberate taste, not a survey: the Sunday flea market and vermouth, the park, maybe a short look at the Prado, and dinner at the world's oldest restaurant.

Monday 6 September — Madrid → Oviedo

~3¼ hours by fast train · optional Segovia stop on the way
Details

The Alvia leaves from Chamartín station and runs under the Cantabrian mountains through one of Europe's longest rail tunnels. Reserved seats, bags beside us. The same line passes Segovia, so if the timetable allows we may spend the middle of the day there — a private driver takes us and the luggage from Madrid to the aqueduct, and drops us at Segovia's station for an afternoon train on to Oviedo. We'll confirm once tickets go on sale; nothing else depends on it.

Oviedo — 4 nights, the northern hub

Three full days fanning out from one hotel — mountains, west coast, and a spa day — with the order decided by the weather, not the plan. The car is collected on Tuesday morning.

Friday 10 September — Oviedo → San Sebastián

~3¾ hours of driving, split by the caves and an anchovy lunch
Details

The trip's longest driving day, built around the one sight on the route with no equivalent anywhere else: original Paleolithic cave art at El Castillo, seen by lamplight in a small group. Then lunch in Santoña beside the motorway, and San Sebastián by late afternoon. Luggage is loaded once in Oviedo and the boot stays shut until the hotel; valuables ride with us in daypacks.

San Sebastián — 3 nights

A weekend in the best casual eating city in Europe, wrapped around the best urban beach. The car stays parked except for one lunch outing to Getaria.

Monday 13 September — San Sebastián → Bilbao

~2½ hours with stops · a gentle coast day
Details

Barely over an hour direct, stretched into a slow coastal day: Gernika on its Monday market day, a portside lunch in Bermeo, the hermitage islet of Gaztelugatxe from its clifftop viewpoint, and into Bilbao by late afternoon.

Bilbao — 2 nights, all four of us

Pintxos in the old town on Monday evening; the Guggenheim, the Ribera market, the Artxanda funicular, and a farewell dinner on Tuesday.

Wednesday 15 September — Mum & Pa fly home

20 minutes to the airport · the car is returned there
Details

We drive Mum & Pa to Bilbao airport for the Lufthansa flight to Frankfurt, hand the rental car back at the terminal, and taxi back into town. In Frankfurt they sleep at a hotel inside the airport complex — no shuttle, no rushing — and board the Singapore Airlines flight home around midday on Thursday.

Bilbao — 2 more nights for X & E

A couple's coda: harbor sardines by metro, the world's first transporter bridge, late pintxos rounds, the Guggenheim interior properly.

Friday 17 September — X & E fly home

Taxi to the airport · mid-afternoon flight via Madrid to Newark
Booking notes (the boring bits, handled)

Flights first: X & E's Newark–Madrid and the Iberia award home; Mum & Pa's Singapore–Frankfurt–Madrid in, and Bilbao–Frankfurt plus the airport hotel and Singapore flight out. Hotels in all four cities are held refundable until the flights are ticketed. The car is a routine one-way within Spain — picked up in Oviedo on Tuesday morning, dropped at Bilbao airport on the Wednesday we take Mum & Pa to their flight — so it never sits in a city garage longer than it must. Train tickets go on sale a few months out and get booked the day they appear (and that's when the Segovia question answers itself). The handful of scarce things — the Getaria table, the cave visit, the Covadonga shuttle, Botín — get booked the moment each one opens; the Guggenheim and the rest a few weeks out. Nothing here requires anyone else to plan anything.

Madrid — the opening weekend

None of us has ever been — and two nights is a deliberate taste, not a survey; the rest of Madrid is a future trip. The calendar hands us the city's best day: a Sunday.

Saturday evening

Plaza Mayor, La Latina, tapas

Hotel by mid-afternoon, then an easy first evening on foot: the great arcaded square, the lanes of La Latina, and tapas on Cava Baja — tortilla, gambas al ajillo, jamón from a proper taberna. Churros con chocolate at San Ginés for anyone still standing.

Sunday

The Rastro, vermouth, and the park

The El Rastro flea market in the morning, then vermouth at the bars around it — the local Sunday ritual. Retiro Park in the afternoon: the Crystal Palace, the boating lake, the paseo.

Optional · if the mood strikes

A short Prado hop

If energy allows, 45–60 minutes for the big three — Velázquez's Las Meninas, Goya, Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights — and out again. Skipped without guilt otherwise: the town is the attraction.

Sunday dinner · booked

Sobrino de Botín

The world's oldest continuously operating restaurant (1725): wood-fired roast suckling pig and lamb, in a dining room that has barely changed. The send-off meal before the train north.

Monday · if the timetable allows

Segovia, on the way north

The high-speed line to Asturias runs right past Segovia, so Monday's travel day may spend its middle well: the Roman aqueduct still standing over the square without a drop of mortar, the old town and cathedral, the Alcázar, and a wood-roasted cochinillo lunch in the aqueduct's shadow. A private driver carries us and the bags from Madrid and drops everyone at Segovia's station for the afternoon train, so the day is effortless. We'll confirm when the 2027 timetable is published.

Oviedo — four nights in cider country's capital

Three hours after leaving Madrid we're in a different Spain: green, granite, and Atlantic. Oviedo is polished, pedestrianized, famously spotless, and quiet by half past eleven — a city in name, a big town in feel, with mountains on the horizon. The town, the market, and the cider culture are the evening program; three full days fan out in three directions.

The evenings

Calle Gascona, the cider boulevard

Waiters pour cider from above their heads to wake it up, the floor is slick with it, and the tables are long and shared. Fabada, fabes con almejas, cachopo if brave. The pouring is the evening's theater — and dinner here may be the best of the trip.

The old town & the mountain kings' chapel

The cathedral square where the oldest Camino begins (pilgrims with shells and staffs at dawn), Plaza del Fontán, the bronze statues, and the El Fontán market for Cabrales cheese and fruit for the hotel room. Ten minutes up the hill by taxi: Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo, UNESCO-listed royal buildings from the 9th century, with the city panorama thrown in.

The three day trips — ordered by weather, not by plan

The clearest forecast

Covadonga & Cangas de Onís — the mountain day

About an hour's drive to Cangas, then a pre-booked shuttle climbs to the glacial lakes of Enol and Ercina at 1,100 m — green water, grazing cows, the western Picos all around, short strolls between viewpoints. The pink basilica and the Holy Cave after, where tradition says the Reconquista began, and lunch in Cangas by its humpback “Roman” bridge. Pitched honestly: history plus landscape. A cloudy morning means we simply float the day — nobody rides a shuttle into fog.

Absorbs a grey day

The west coast — Cudillero

Forty-five minutes to a fishing village stacked like an amphitheater around its harbor, for a caldereta fish-stew lunch — arrive by one, linger after the day-trippers thin. Optionally on to Luarca's white town and working port.

The recovery day

City & spa

Oviedo on foot in the morning, Naranco by taxi, and the afternoon soaking at the Las Caldas thermal spa, fifteen minutes out of town. The no-car day, and Mum & Pa's to shape.

In reserve

If a day falls through

The east coast — the cliff-village viewpoint above Lastres, the inland beach at Gulpiyuri, the old quarter and port of Llanes — or Ribadesella and the Tito Bustillo cave, the fallback if Friday's cave visit can't be booked.

Friday — the caves, en route to the coast

Moving day, done cleverly: the longest drive of the trip carries the one sight on the route that has no equivalent anywhere else in the family's travels. Luggage loaded once in Oviedo, a late-morning appointment underground, lunch in the anchovy capital, and San Sebastián by late afternoon.

Late morning · booked

El Castillo, Puente Viesgo

Original Paleolithic art, not a replica: hand stencils and red discs dated to around 40,000 years, among the oldest cave paintings on Earth, plus bison and horses — seen by lamplight in a group of about a dozen, less than two hours from Oviedo and just off the motorway. (Altamira is the famous one, but its public version is a copy.)

Lunch

Santoña, the anchovy capital

Forty minutes on, beside the motorway: the town that cures the world's best anchovies. A pintxo-and-rabas lunch, and shops for tins to carry home.

Then the coast

Another hour and three-quarters east and we're in San Sebastián for the weekend. The car goes into the hotel garage and, apart from one lunch outing, stays there.

San Sebastián — a weekend by the bay

The best eating city in Europe at the casual register, wrapped around the best urban beach — a belle-époque seaside town in feel, flat and walkable corner to corner, and the most parent-friendly base of the four by day. We have it over a weekend, when the pintxos scene is at full strength, and the sea is at its warmest of the year.

La Concha & the Igueldo funicular

The promenade end to end, a swim if September obliges, and the antique funicular up Monte Igueldo to the belle-époque overlook of the whole bay at golden hour.

Pintxos, two quarters

The Parte Vieja classics — the gilda, anchovy toasts, bites of txuleta — and Gros across the river, the locals' quarter with less crush. We start early: between seven and eight the bars are relaxed and there's room at the counter; the half-past-nine squeeze is for later rounds.

Saturday or Sunday lunch · the booked table of the trip

Getaria — whole turbot over coals

Half an hour west, on the harbor grill row: whole wild turbot over charcoal at Elkano, the restaurant that defined the dish — the purest product-first fish cooking in Spain, the Cantonese steamed-fish ideal translated to fire. All four of us at the table. Txakoli from the hill above the village to go with it.

Zumaia's flysch, on the way back

Fifteen minutes from Getaria: cliffs of tilted rock strata stacked like a filing cabinet of geological time, with a short, flat path to the viewpoint.

The other day · pick one or none

Pasaia, Hondarribia, or the beach

Pasaia is a fishing-port district twenty minutes out, with a harbor-mouth fish lunch and a two-minute ferry across the channel. Hondarribia is the walled border town facing France, with marina-quarter pintxos. Or simply the beach.

Monday — the Basque coast, en route to Bilbao

Barely over an hour direct, so we take the slow way: a full, gentle day along the Urdaibai coast with the car, never far from the next stop.

Morning

Gernika on market day

Monday is the town's weekly market day — the living version of the market Picasso's painting depicts — plus the Basque assembly house and its famous oak.

Lunch

Bermeo

Twenty minutes on, a real working fishing port with tall painted houses stacked above the boats. A simple portside lunch among them.

Afternoon

Gaztelugatxe

Ten minutes from Bermeo: the hermitage islet on its winding stone causeway. The clifftop viewpoint needs no effort at all; the 240-odd steps down and across are strictly optional. Into Bilbao by late afternoon, forty minutes on.

Bilbao — the finale, then a coda

A former industrial city reborn around one astonishing building, with the best casual eating of the trip. We base on the old-town side for the compact, village-like register. Two nights all together — then Mum & Pa fly home from an airport twenty minutes away, and X & E stay two more.

Monday evening

The Casco Viejo, grazing

The Siete Calles and the arcaded Plaza Nueva, ringed with pintxos bars — Basque counter-top eating at its best, a few steps from the hotel.

Tuesday

The Guggenheim, the market, the balcony

Gehry's titanium ship on the river — the one planned museum interior of the trip, and worth it for the building alone; the flower Puppy stands guard outside and the riverfront walk is lovely either way. Lunch under the roof of the Ribera market, Europe's largest covered market, and the little Artxanda funicular up to the city's golden-hour balcony. Then a farewell dinner: a traditional Bilbao asador, or the harbor run below. Official site.

Wednesday morning

Mum & Pa to the airport

Twenty minutes in the rental car to the terminal, the car handed back there, and a Lufthansa flight to Frankfurt — where a hotel inside the airport complex and a midday Singapore flight the next day make the journey home a calm two-step.

The coda · Wednesday & Thursday

Harbor steps and a flying gondola

By metro to Algorta's old port for anchovies and rabas on the harbor steps, or to Santurtzi for grilled sardines — and between them the UNESCO-listed Vizcaya Bridge, the world's first transporter bridge (1893), crossed hanging from its gondola for pocket change. Plus the Guggenheim interior properly, late pintxos rounds, and the Gaztelugatxe steps if they were skipped on Monday. Friday, a taxi to the airport for the mid-afternoon flight home.

How we'll eat

One idea at the table the whole way: classic dishes, great ingredients, zero pretense — and above all, fish fresh, whole, and simple: salt, fire or coals, nothing clever. The route runs capital classics → cider country → coast → craft.

The route, in dishes

Tapas on Cava Baja, vermouth after the Rastro, and a 300-year-old roast house in Madrid · wood-roasted suckling pig in Segovia, if the stopover happens · cider poured from arm's length with fabada on a street built entirely of cider houses · caldereta in Cudillero's harbor amphitheater · the world's best anchovies in Santoña, tins in the luggage · two pintxos quarters over a weekend, and Getaria's whole turbot over coals with all four of us at the table · a portside lunch in Bermeo · and in Bilbao: pintxos counters, the Ribera market, and sardines on the harbor steps with a bridge-gondola ride between courses.

Not one tasting menu

Reservations exist only where walk-ins fail: Botín in Madrid and Elkano in Getaria are the two booked tables, and the cave visit, the Covadonga shuttle, and the Guggenheim the only other appointments. Everything else walks in the door — because where we're going, that's how the best food works.

The greens layer

Learned on previous two-week trips: Spain won't volunteer vegetables, so we order them deliberately. Two standing family rules — at least two vegetable first courses for the table at every sit-down dinner (salads, soups, peppers; that's where the vegetables live), and market fruit in the hotel room at every base: September is peak figs, peaches, and grapes. In the Basque Country, menestra de verduras, the braised-vegetable masterpiece, gets ordered on sight; on pintxos nights we lean on gilda and pepper pintxos and make lunch the salad-forward meal.

Good questions, honest answers

How much walking is involved?

Moderate, and always at our own pace. Every base is a compact, flat city; the mountains come to us by shuttle at Covadonga; the funiculars in San Sebastián and Bilbao do the climbing for the views; and the famous Gaztelugatxe steps are strictly optional — the clifftop viewpoint needs none. Museums are optional throughout, so there are no long gallery days.

Is the driving hard?

No car at all for the first three days. Then three day trips from Oviedo, each about an hour out on good roads, and two transfer days that are really sightseeing days with the luggage in the boot: Friday is the longest at under four hours of driving, broken by the caves and lunch; Monday is barely over an hour direct, taken slowly along the coast. The car's last job is delivering Mum & Pa to the airport door.

What about language and money?

English works fine in hotels, museums, and cities; in village restaurants a pointed finger and a smile order beautifully (and X can handle the menu Spanish). Cards are accepted everywhere — no one needs to carry meaningful cash.

What's the weather like in early September?

Madrid may still touch 30 °C; the trip cools as it goes north. The north coast is “Green Spain” — green because it rains — but early September is its driest, warmest window, with the sea at its year-round warmest and the Spanish school holidays over on September 1, so the crowds drain out as we arrive. We pack a layer and a light rain jacket, and the Oviedo days reshuffle around the forecast: the clearest morning gets the mountains, a grey one gets the fishing village, and the spa day fits anywhere.

Aren't the flights home complicated?

Not in practice. Mum & Pa fly Bilbao to Frankfurt on Wednesday afternoon, sleep at a hotel inside the airport complex — a calm evening, no dawn sprints — and board the Singapore flight around midday on Thursday; the overnight is the buffer. X & E fly Bilbao → Madrid → Newark on Friday; we're aiming to have the Bilbao hop on the same ticket as the long flight so the connection is protected and bags go straight through, and if not, we'll leave a generous gap in Madrid.

Is the plan final?

The dates and the four bases are fixed. Two small things stay open on purpose: whether Monday's train day includes the Segovia stop depends on the 2027 timetable, and whether the Getaria lunch is Saturday or Sunday depends on the restaurant's opening days. Neither changes anything else. Hotels are held refundable until the flights are ticketed.

What still needs to be confirmed?

Exact train times once tickets go on sale, the Covadonga shuttle timetable when the season's plan publishes in spring, the cave visit's slot, the rental car's one-way fee, the Lufthansa Wednesday schedule, and the usual timed tickets. None of it changes the shape of the trip — and none of it is anyone's job but X's.
