# Family Trip — Destinations & Things to Do

*Companion to the logistics document. Route order: Madrid → (fast train, optional Segovia stop) → Oviedo → (via the Cantabrian caves) → San Sebastián → (via Gernika) → Bilbao. Sep 4–17, 2027. Urban nights, village days: every base is a compact, walkable city; the fishing towns, mountain lakes, and stone villages are collected on day trips and transfer days. Parents depart from Bilbao on Wed Sep 15; the last two Bilbao days are a couple's coda.*

*Food philosophy for the whole trip: unpretentious classics and great ingredients, zero Michelin-chasing — and specifically **product-first fish**: whole fresh fish, salt, fire or coals, nothing clever. Museums are optional throughout: a short Prado hop if the mood strikes, the Guggenheim as the one planned interior; everywhere else, the town is the attraction.*

---

## Madrid (2 nights — Sat, Sun)

Nobody in the family has been. Two nights is a deliberate taste, not a survey — the rest of Madrid is a future trip.

- **Saturday (arrival):** land ~13:20, hotel by ~15:00, parents in from Frankfurt the same afternoon. Easy first evening: **Plaza Mayor → the lanes of La Latina → Cava Baja tapas** — tortilla, gambas al ajillo, jamón from a proper taberna. **Churros con chocolate at San Ginés** if anyone's still standing.
- **Sunday — the one full day:**
  - **El Rastro** flea market in La Latina, then **vermouth at the surrounding bars** — the local Sunday ritual, and the calendar hands it to us.
  - **Retiro Park** in the afternoon — the Crystal Palace, the boating lake, the paseo.
  - **The Prado — optional short hop.** If energy allows, 45–60 minutes for the big three (Velázquez's *Las Meninas*, Goya, Bosch's *Garden of Earthly Delights*); skip without guilt.
  - **Dinner at Sobrino de Botín** — the world's oldest continuously operating restaurant (1725): wood-fired roast suckling pig and lamb. The send-off meal, booked ahead.

## Monday: Madrid → Oviedo by train (with an optional Segovia stop)

Monday was always a travel day; the Segovia option simply spends the middle of it well and gets us into Oviedo in the evening instead of mid-afternoon.

- **Default:** Alvia from **Chamartín** (not Atocha), ~3h15 via the Pajares base tunnel. Arrive Oviedo early afternoon, first sidrería dinner.
- **Segovia stopover option:** the Madrid–Asturias high-speed line runs *through* **Segovia-Guiomar**, ~27 min from Chamartín, and some Asturias-bound services stop there. So: morning to Segovia, **4–5 hours in town** — the **Roman aqueduct** (still standing over the square, no mortar), the old town and cathedral, the **Alcázar**, and a **wood-roasted *cochinillo* lunch** in the aqueduct's shadow — then an afternoon train on to Oviedo, arriving early evening.
  - **The catch is luggage:** Guiomar is ~7 km outside town, and four people's suitcases need to go somewhere for the day. The low-friction version: a **private driver Madrid → Segovia** with the bags in the car (~1h), the day in town, driver drops everyone and the bags at Guiomar for the onward train. Pay-for-comfort territory, and it makes the day effortless for the parents.
  - Decide once Renfe publishes the timetable: only worth it if an afternoon Segovia→Oviedo service fits (see logistics).

## Oviedo (4 nights — Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu) — the northern hub

Asturias' elegant little capital: a polished, pedestrianized, famously spotless old town, the spiritual home of Spanish cider, and three full days fanning out in three directions. City in name, big-town in feel — quiet by 11:30, mountains on the horizon. **No museums — the town, the market, and the cider culture are the program.**

**In town, threaded through the evenings:**
- **Calle Gascona, "the cider boulevard"** — sidrerías where waiters pour from above their heads (*escanciar*), the floor slick with it, long shared tables. *Fabada*, *fabes con almejas*, *cachopo* if brave. The pouring is the evening's theater.
- **The old town** — cathedral square (where the Camino Primitivo starts — shell-and-staff pilgrims at dawn), Plaza del Fontán, the bronze statues, **Mercado El Fontán** (Cabrales and the cheese counter; fruit for the hotel room).
- **Monte Naranco** — Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo, UNESCO 9th-century pre-Romanesque royal buildings. 10 min by taxi, city panorama included.

**The three day trips (ordered by weather, not by plan):**

1. **Covadonga + Cangas de Onís — the mountain day (takes the clearest forecast).** Drive ~1h05, park at the Cangas bus station, **Alsa shuttle** up to the Lakes of Covadonga (Enol and Ercina, ~1,100 m — glacial water, grazing cows, the western Picos all around; short strolls between viewpoints). Basilica and Holy Cave after — the pink church in its mountain amphitheater, where tradition says the Reconquista began. Lunch in Cangas by the "Roman bridge." Pitched honestly: history plus landscape, not a lakes spectacle. *(Road closed to private cars in season; shuttle booked in advance. Bad morning = don't ride a shuttle into cloud; float the day.)*
2. **The west coast — Cudillero (+ Luarca).** ~45 min. Cudillero's amphitheater harbor for the *caldereta* lunch — arrive by 1:00, linger after the day-trippers thin. Optionally on to Luarca's white town and working port. Absorbs a grey day gracefully.
3. **City + spa day — the recovery day.** Oviedo on foot in the morning, Naranco by taxi, then **Las Caldas thermal spa** (~15 min out) for the afternoon soak. The parents' no-car day.

**Bench (if a day falls through or appetites run high):** the east coast — San Roque viewpoint above Lastres, Playa de Gulpiyuri (high-ish tide only), Llanes old quarter and port; or Ribadesella and **Tito Bustillo** cave (original Paleolithic art — the fallback if the Friday caves slot doesn't work out).

## Friday transfer: Oviedo → San Sebastián (via the caves)

The trip's longest driving day (~3h45 total), carrying the one sight on the route with no equivalent anywhere else in the family's travels:

- **El Castillo caves, Puente Viesgo** (~1h45 from Oviedo, just off the A-8) — late-morning slot. *Original* Paleolithic art, not a replica: hand stencils and red discs at ~40,000 years, among the oldest dated cave art on Earth, plus bison and horses, seen by lamplight in a group of about a dozen. (Altamira is the famous one, but its public version is a copy.)
- **Lunch at Santoña** (~40 min on, beside the A-8) — the anchovy capital of the world: shops for tins to carry home, a pintxo-and-*rabas* lunch.
- San Sebastián by late afternoon.

## San Sebastián / Donostia (3 nights — Fri, Sat, Sun; all four together)

The best eating city in Europe at the casual register, wrapped around the best urban beach — belle-époque seaside town in feel, flat and walkable corner to corner, and the most parent-friendly base of the four by day. We have it over a weekend, when the pintxos scene is at full strength. Car stays parked.

- **La Concha** — the promenade end to end; a swim if September obliges (the sea at its annual warmest).
- **Monte Igueldo funicular** — the antique funicular to the belle-époque overlook of the bay. Golden hour.
- **Pintxos, two quarters:** the **Parte Vieja** classics (gilda, anchovy toasts, txuleta bites) and **Gros** across the river (the locals' quarter, less crush). With the parents, start early — 19:00–20:00 the bars are relaxed and there's room at the counter; the 21:30 crush is for later rounds.
- **Saturday or Sunday — Getaria + Zumaia, the booked lunch of the trip** (~25–30 min west): whole wild turbot over coals on the harbor grill row — **Elkano**, the restaurant that defined the dish (book the moment reservations open; **Kaia Kaipe** two doors up is the less-ceremonial alternative). The purest product-first fish cooking in Spain — the Cantonese-steamed-fish ideal, translated to charcoal. Txakoli from the hill above town, then **Zumaia's flysch cliffs** (tilted rock strata like a filing cabinet of geological time) on the way back.
- **The other day, pick one or none:** **Pasaia** — fishing-port district 20 min out, harbor-mouth fish lunch, the two-minute ferry across the channel; or **Hondarribia** — the walled border town facing France, marina-quarter pintxos. Or simply the beach.

## Monday transfer: San Sebastián → Bilbao (via the Urdaibai coast)

~1h15 direct; routed as a full, gentle coast day with the car:

- **Gernika** (~1h) — **Monday is the town's weekly market day**, the living version of the market Picasso's painting depicts; plus the Basque assembly house and its oak.
- **Bermeo** (20 min on) — a real working fishing port; simple portside lunch.
- **Gaztelugatxe** (10 min) — the hermitage islet on its winding stone causeway. The clifftop viewpoint needs no effort; the ~240 steps each way are optional (free timed permit required in season if going down).
- Into Bilbao (~40 min) by late afternoon.

## Bilbao (Mon–Fri: 2 nights with the parents, then a 2-night couple's coda)

A former industrial city reborn around one building, with the best casual eating of the trip. Base on the Casco Viejo side for the compact, town-like register. BIO airport is ~12 km out — a 15–25 minute taxi.

**With the parents (Mon evening, Tue):**
- **Monday evening: Casco Viejo pintxos** — the Siete Calles and arcaded Plaza Nueva.
- **Tuesday: the Guggenheim** (open Tuesdays; parents' interest optional — Gehry's titanium exterior, the Puppy, and the riverfront walk are the point either way), **Mercado de la Ribera** for lunch (Europe's largest covered market), **Artxanda funicular** for the golden-hour city balcony. Farewell dinner: a traditional Bilbao asador or the casual-harbor run below.
- **Wednesday morning:** parents to BIO (20 min).

**The couple's coda (Wed, Thu):**
- **The casual-harbor evening:** metro to **Algorta's Puerto Viejo** (anchovies and *rabas* on the harbor steps) or **Santurtzi** (grilled sardines), crossing the **Vizcaya Bridge** — the world's first transporter bridge (UNESCO, 1893) — hanging from its gondola for pocket change between them.
- The Guggenheim interior properly, if it got the short version with the parents; late pintxos rounds; the Gaztelugatxe steps if skipped on Monday.
- **Friday:** taxi to BIO for the mid-afternoon flight.

## How we eat (the through-line)

**Classic dishes, great ingredients, zero pretense — fish fresh, whole, and simple.** The route runs capital classics → cider country → coast → craft:

- **Madrid:** tapas on Cava Baja, vermouth after the Rastro, one 300-year-old roast house (Botín), churros at midnight.
- **Segovia (if the stopover happens):** wood-roasted *cochinillo* in the aqueduct's shadow.
- **Oviedo:** cider poured from arm's length, *fabada*, *fabes con almejas* — dinner on a street built entirely of cider houses.
- **Cudillero (day trip):** *caldereta* in the harbor amphitheater.
- **Santoña (in transit):** the world's best anchovies; tins in the luggage.
- **San Sebastián:** two pintxos quarters over a weekend — and **Getaria's whole turbot over coals, the one big booked table**, with all four of us at it.
- **Bermeo (in transit):** portside lunch among the fishing boats.
- **Bilbao:** pintxos counters, the Ribera market, sardines or anchovies on the harbor steps with a UNESCO bridge-gondola ride between courses.

Not one tasting menu in the whole trip. Reservations exist only where walk-ins fail: **Botín, Elkano (book early), the El Castillo caves, the Covadonga shuttle, and the Guggenheim.**

**The greens layer** *(learned the hard way on previous two-week trips: Spain won't volunteer vegetables — order them deliberately)*. Standing family rules: **every sit-down dinner, at least two vegetable primeros for the table** (the first-course section is where the vegetables live — salads, soups, peppers; don't skip to the protein), and **keep market fruit in the hotel room** at every base (September = peak figs, peaches, grapes). Per region:

- **Madrid:** *ensalada mixta*, gazpacho/salmorejo while the heat lasts, *pisto*, grilled asparagus.
- **Oviedo (the thinnest stretch — compensate hardest):** *ensalada mixta* as a default primero, Fontán market fruit run on day one.
- **Cantabrian coast stops:** September tomato salad — peak month, order it plain with oil and salt.
- **San Sebastián & Bilbao (the best window):** Gernika peppers in season, *piquillos*, *porrusalda*, grilled leeks — and above all **menestra de verduras** (the Navarran braised-vegetable masterpiece): order it on sight.
- **Pintxos nights are the lowest-fiber by design** — lean on gilda and pepper pintxos, and make those days' lunches the salad-forward ones.

## The trip in one line

A Madrid weekend, a fast train under the mountains (with a Roman aqueduct on the way, if we like), four nights in the cider capital, 40,000-year-old hands on a cave wall, a whole turbot over coals, and the Basque coast to finish — urban nights, village days, and the best unpretentious eating in Europe the entire way.
