# Prompt: Trip-Planning Website Generator — Northern Spain

Reference the two attached documents (`trip-logistics.md` and `trip-activities.md`) for more information when building the website.

---

I'm planning a multi-generational family trip through Madrid and "Green Spain" (Asturias, Cantabria, the Basque Country), Sep 4–17, 2027, and I've attached two documents: one covering all logistics (flights, the fast train, the one-way rental car, nights per stop) and one covering the destinations and activities. Please build a single-page trip-planning website from them.

**Before building anything: review both documents and check their assumptions.** Flag anything that looks inconsistent, outdated, or worth verifying (train times, drive times, access restrictions like the Covadonga lakes shuttle, cave-visit group caps, booking lead times, etc.) and list your findings first. Where the documents say "verify," the site should say "we'll confirm the details" rather than inventing specifics. Only after that review, build the site.

**Audience:** primarily my parents (60s–70s, flying from Singapore via Frankfurt). Nobody in the family has been to Madrid or northern Spain, so the whole route is new — but don't oversell it as exotic; the tone is "this will be beautiful, delicious, and easy." The family food philosophy matters and should color the food content: unpretentious classics and great ingredients, zero Michelin-chasing — and specifically product-first fish (whole fresh fish, salt, fire or coals). The one big booked table is Elkano's whole turbot in Getaria; San Sebastián's and Bilbao's pintxos are the other Basque food flags. The "greens layer" (two vegetable primeros at every sit-down dinner, market fruit in the hotel room) is a real family rule — present it warmly. Secondary: reassure everyone that the driving is short and gentle (two transfer days of ~2.5–3.75 h built around sightseeing stops, Oviedo day trips ≤2h15 round trip) and that mountain days need no fitness (the Covadonga shuttle does the climbing). "Urban nights, village days" is the shape: every base is a compact walkable city; the villages, lakes and fishing ports are collected on day trips and transfer days. The group splits once, at a 20-minute airport run, and the last two Bilbao days are a couple's coda — present that lightly. The site's job is buy-in: get everyone excited and confident that the logistics are smooth and taken care of. Warm and reassuring in tone, not a booking tool. English, generous font sizes and high contrast, fully mobile-friendly (they'll view it on phones).

**Structure:**
1. A hero section with the trip's one-line arc and a route overview — an elegant step-by-step visual of the stops (Madrid 2 → Oviedo 4 → San Sebastián 3 → Bilbao 2 + 2) with nights at each. No map needed.
2. A "how we'll travel" section that reframes the logistics as comfort: no car until Oviedo (city days + one fast train under the mountains, with an optional Segovia stop), then one car picked up Tuesday morning, three weather-ordered day trips from Oviedo, two driving transfers that double as sightseeing days (the caves + Santoña; Gernika + Bermeo + Gaztelugatxe), and the car dropped at the airport when the parents fly. A simple visual day-by-day timeline with real dates works well here. Mention the weather rules as a feature (Oviedo day trips ordered by forecast; no shuttle into cloud) and the still-open items (the Segovia stopover depends on the timetable; Elkano's closing days decide Saturday vs Sunday).
3. One section per destination, in route order, drawing from the activities document: a short evocative intro, then highlights as scannable cards. Each transfer day (Friday caves, Monday Urdaibai coast) deserves its own short section. Museums are optional throughout — a short Prado hop if the mood strikes, the Guggenheim as the one planned interior — and the site should present the light-museum plan as a deliberate feature. Include a photo for each destination (royalty-free Wikimedia Commons images downloaded locally with a credits section, as on the Alps site) — key subjects: the Lakes of Covadonga, Plaza Mayor, the Segovia aqueduct, cider pouring in Oviedo, the Covadonga basilica, Cudillero's harbor, El Castillo cave art, Santoña anchovies, La Concha bay, a pintxos counter, Getaria's harbor, the Zumaia flysch, Bermeo, Gaztelugatxe, the Guggenheim, the Vizcaya Bridge.
4. A "how we'll eat" section (standalone or woven into the flow — builder's choice, but it must exist): the family food philosophy and the food through-line of the route, from tapas and Botín to cider houses, Santoña anchovies, Getaria's turbot, pintxos in two cities, and harbor sardines in Bilbao — plus the greens layer.
5. A short FAQ addressing likely concerns: How much walking? (moderate, own pace, flat cities, the shuttle does the climbing) Is the driving hard? (none for the first three days; two transfer days built around stops; short day trips) Language/money? (English fine in cities, cards everywhere) September weather? (the driest window Green Spain offers, but pack a light rain layer — the Oviedo days reshuffle around it; Madrid may still be ~30°C) What about the flights home? (parents: Bilbao → Frankfurt, airport-hotel night, Singapore next day; X & E: Bilbao → Madrid → Newark with a protected or generous connection) Is the plan final? (dates fixed; Segovia and the Getaria day are the open items; hotels held refundable until flights are ticketed).
6. External links where natural: Renfe, the Prado, the Guggenheim, Gaztelugatxe permits — opened in new tabs.

**Design:** same design language as the Alps site (`site/alps-2027/index.html`): cream/ink/alpine palette, serif headings, card grid, sticky section nav, lightbox, mobile-first. Everything self-contained in one HTML file with local images, relative paths, and `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`.

Do not invent train times, prices, or hotel names beyond what the documents contain. Accuracy over embellishment.
